About the Research

The science behind
novelty and aversion

A multidisciplinary study combining consumer psychology, AI-assisted image classification, and generational analysis — conducted at the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore.

Background
Why neophilia & neophobia?

Neophilia — the attraction to novelty — and neophobia — the aversion to unfamiliar stimuli — are well-documented psychological traits that influence consumer behaviour across domains. In product design, these tendencies determine which innovations succeed in the market and which fail despite technical merit.

As product design increasingly diverges from conventional forms — driven by AI-assisted generative design, 3D printing, and global trend convergence — understanding where different populations draw the line between fascination and rejection becomes commercially and academically critical.

This study is the first to examine this boundary systematically across four distinct generational cohorts using a hybrid AI + human evaluation methodology.

Research Framework
EUPHORIA-RETINA Framework

The AI classification pipeline used in this study is built on the EUPHORIA-RETINA framework (Sankar & Sen, 2025), a gaze-derived preference signature system developed at IISc Bangalore.

We extend this framework with an image embedding pipeline using Google's Gemini Embedding API to compute visual dissimilarity scores for each product design against a reference dataset of 100 conventional products per category.

The final classification combines our pipeline score with LLM-based scoring (Gemini 3, ChatGPT, Claude) using a 50/50 weighted average — achieving 67% alignment with human ground-truth classification across tested product categories.

Methodology
The AI Classification Pipeline
A
Image Scraping
Mass-market product images scraped from Bing using 12 targeted queries per product category. Focuses on everyday, affordable designs to represent the conventional design space.
icrawler · Bing Image Search
B
CLIP-Based Filtering
Each image passes through 5 CLIP zero-shot classification gates — relevance, object count, background quality, viewpoint, and conventionality — to ensure only clean, product-focused images enter the reference dataset.
OpenAI CLIP · Zero-shot classification
C
Preprocessing
All images are resized to 512×512 px with centre-crop aspect preservation, stripped of metadata, and saved as PNG for consistent embedding input.
Pillow · Centre-crop resize
D
Image Embedding
Each image is embedded as a high-dimensional vector using Gemini's vision embedding model, capturing shape, colour, and texture as they appear — no text descriptions involved.
gemini-embedding-2-preview
E
Calibration
Pairwise cosine dissimilarity between all 100 reference images establishes μ₀* (conventional baseline) and σ̄* (spread baseline), computed once and frozen for all subsequent scoring.
Cosine dissimilarity · P₂₀ baseline
F–G
Scoring
Each test product is compared against all 100 reference images. The resulting μ and σ are passed through the v4 formula to produce a final unconventionality score U ∈ [0, 1].
Custom formula · Sigmoid activation
Unconventionality Formula (v4)
raw = α × (μ_img − μ₀*)
boost = max(1 + β × (σ̄* − σ_img) / σ̄*, 0.1)
arg = raw × boost
U_img = sigmoid(arg)

// Parameters
α = 26.0 // mean sensitivity
β = 1.5 // boost sensitivity
μ₀* // frozen from reference dataset
σ̄* // frozen from reference dataset

Ethics & Privacy
Your data is safe with us

This study adheres to standard academic research ethics principles. Participation is entirely voluntary and anonymous.

We do not collect IP addresses, browser fingerprints, cookies, or any device identifiers. The only personal information collected is what you voluntarily submit in the survey form.

🎓

Data will be used solely for academic research purposes — publication in peer-reviewed journals and presentation at design research conferences. No commercial use of any kind.
Study Design
Six products. Three archetypes. Four generations.

Each product category was selected to represent a common everyday object with a well-established conventional form — making deviations from that form clearly detectable by both the AI pipeline and human evaluators. Within each category, three stimulus images represent the Conventional, Hybrid, and Unconventional archetypes as defined by the pipeline score.

Product image order is randomised per participant (A/B/C label assignment is random) to eliminate position bias. No archetype labels are shown to participants during the survey — they are shown only neutral letter labels (A, B, C).
