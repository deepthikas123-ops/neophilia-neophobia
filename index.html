About the Research

Mapping the boundary between
fascination and aversion

Every product design sits somewhere on a spectrum — from deeply familiar to boldly novel. This study investigates where different generations draw the line between neophilia (attraction to novelty) and neophobia (aversion to novelty) in everyday product design.

Using a custom AI pipeline developed at IISc Bangalore, we classify product designs into three archetypes — Conventional, Hybrid, and Unconventional — and ask participants to make purchase choices based purely on visual appearance. Your responses will directly inform how designers and brands think about product innovation across different market segments.

How It Works

Simple. Visual. ~27 minutes.

01
Read & Consent
Review the study purpose, your rights as a participant, and give informed consent before we begin.
02
Your Background
Share basic demographic details — age, gender, occupation, income range. Used only for analysis grouping.
03
Choose & Rank
For each of the 6 products, view 3 design options and make purchase choices based solely on appearance.

Rate & Reflect
Rate your likelihood to buy each design and answer a few questions about your general design preferences.

Ready to participate?

What this study is about: You will be shown images of everyday products in three design variations — conventional, hybrid, and unconventional. You will make purchase decisions based on appearance alone.

Time required: Approximately 27–30 minutes. You can pause and return — your progress is saved automatically.

Your data: No identifying information is linked to your responses. Your date of birth is used only to assign you to a generational group. We do not collect IP addresses, cookies, or device identifiers.

Voluntary participation: Participation is entirely voluntary. You may withdraw at any time without consequence by closing the browser window.

Contact: For questions about this study, contact the research team at IISc Bangalore, Department of Design & Mechanical Engineering.
